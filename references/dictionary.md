# ASD-STE100 - Dictionary, Technical Nouns, and Technical Verbs (Part 2)

This file covers the controlled vocabulary system. Use it during Step 2 analysis (checking each word) and Step 3 rewriting (finding approved replacements).

**Copyright note:** The ASD-STE100 dictionary (875 approved words, 1,274 non-approved words with alternatives) is copyrighted by ASD (EU Trade Mark 017966390). This file describes the structure and principles so you can apply them. For the exact dictionary, download the official standard free at asd-ste100.org. Do not reproduce the dictionary verbatim.

---

## The 4-Column Dictionary Format

Each dictionary entry has four columns:

| Column | Content | Format |
|---|---|---|
| **Word (part of speech)** | The word with its approved part of speech | UPPERCASE = approved (use this word); lowercase = not approved (do not use) |
| **Approved meaning / Alternatives** | The single approved meaning, or approved replacements | UPPERCASE for approved meanings/alternatives |
| **STE example** | Correct usage in a sentence | - |
| **Non-STE example** | How the word is misused in standard English | Shows what to avoid |

Lookup sequence:
1. Find the word in the dictionary
2. If it is UPPERCASE (approved), verify the part of speech matches your usage
3. If it is lowercase (not approved), take the UPPERCASE alternative from column 2
4. Verify the approved meaning matches your intended meaning
5. If the alternative changes the meaning, change the sentence construction

Approved words may have narrower meanings than standard English. For example, "close" (v) has exactly two approved meanings: "move together to stop passage" and "operate a circuit breaker". Not approved for "close a meeting" or "close a business."

---

## The Core Principle

> One word, one meaning, one part of speech.

Every approved word has exactly one meaning and one part of speech. This is the most important rule in the standard. It eliminates lexical ambiguity - the reader never has to guess which sense of a word is intended.

**Examples:**
- `start` (v) - approved. `begin`, `commence`, `initiate` - not approved.
- `fall` (v) - approved meaning: "move down by force of gravity" only. Not "decrease."
- `check` (n) - approved as a noun only. Not approved as a verb. Use "do a check" or "make sure."
- `about` (prep) - approved meaning: "concerned with" only. For the "approximately [a number]" sense, use APPROXIMATELY (adv). For the "around an axis" sense, use AROUND (prep).
- `follow` (v) - approved meaning: "come after, go after." Not "obey." Use "obey" for instructions.
- `close` (v) - approved meanings only: (1) move together to stop passage, (2) operate a circuit breaker.
- `oil` (n) - approved as a noun. Not approved as a verb. Use "apply oil."

## Technical Nouns (Rule 1.5)

A technical noun is a noun term that refers to a specified concept and is applicable to a subject field. The STE dictionary does not include technical nouns because each industry uses different ones.

A non-approved word becomes usable if it fits one of the 22 categories below. If it does not fit any category, it must be replaced with an approved alternative.

### 22 Technical Noun Categories

**1. Official parts information**
Terms from illustrated parts catalogs, engineering drawings, and design documents.
Examples: bolt, cable, clip, conductor, contact, engine, ferry tank, filter, hatch, indicator, light, logo, oil seal, pipe, propeller, screw, switch, transceiver

**2. Vehicles or machines, and locations on them**
All types of vehicles, machines, and their locations.
Examples: aircraft, airframe, cabin, cockpit, cargo compartment, deck, engine room, fuselage, galley, helicopter, wing, wing root

**3. Tools and support equipment, their parts, and locations**
All types of tools, support equipment, parts, and locations.
Examples: access ladder, blade, brush, clamp, cover, display, drill, file, gauge (gage), handle, jack, label, rigging pin, rope, shaft, stand, tag, test rig, torque wrench

**4. Materials, consumables, and unwanted material**
Materials, consumables, and substances that cause contamination.
Examples: acid, adhesive, aluminum alloy, ammunition, compound, copper, debris, detergent, dirt, dust, foam, fuel, grease, oil, paint, plastic, primer, sealant, tape, waste, water

**5. Facilities, infrastructure, and logistic procedures**
Physical facilities, infrastructure systems, and logistic workflows.
Examples: airport, apron, base, building, dock, hangar, port, service bay, shop, storage, transport

**6. Systems, components and circuits, their functions and configurations**
Structure, operation, and design of systems.
Examples: air conditioning, armament, audio, exhaust, flight management, hardware, inlet, pedal, power unit, pump, valve, vent

**7. Mathematical, scientific, engineering terms, and formulas**
Concepts, design, calculations, and methodologies.
Examples: acceleration, capacitance, coefficient, curve, diameter, energy, force, gravity, load, measurement, pressure, temperature, torque, voltage

**8. Navigation and geographic terms**
Positions, directions, and spatial orientation.
Examples: air, altitude, attitude, axis, bank, clearance, climb, coordinates, heading, landing, north, pitch, roll

**9. Numbers, units of measurement, and time**
Quantitative data, measurements, and time.
Examples: 92, 303, ampere (A), degree (°), hour (h), kilogram (kg), knot, liter (L), meter (m), mile, month, ohm (Ω), second (s), year, zero

**10. Quoted text**
Text on placards, labels, signs, markings, and displays.
Examples: EXIT sign, FASTEN SAFETY BELT sign, NO STEP marking, ON position, FAULT legend

**11. Professional roles, individuals, groups, organizations**
Professional functions, persons, organizations, companies, and entities.
Examples: air traffic control, captain, copilot, crew, manufacturer, operator, Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)

**12. Parts of the body**
Anatomical features and ergonomic contexts.
Examples: blood, ear, eyes, hand, head, lung, mouth, skin, stomach

**13. Common personal effects, food, and beverages**
Everyday personal items and consumables.
Examples: clothing, coffee, food, matches, milk, water, wine

**14. Medical terms**
Medical conditions, procedures, and anatomical structures.
Examples: allergy, aspirin, asthma, circulation, diabetes, headache, heart rate, medication, nausea, pulse

**15. Official documents, parts of documentation, standards, and guidelines**
Manuals, technical records, standards, specifications, and regulations.
Examples: Acceptance Test, Checklist, data module, diagram, figure, Maintenance Practice, Service Bulletin, Structural Repair Manual, warning

**16. Environmental and operational conditions**
External factors and operating parameters.
Examples: atmosphere, cloud, daylight, hail, humidity, ice, lightning, rain, snow, storm, turbulence, wind

**17. Colors**
Color names as technical nouns (not adjectives in STE).
Examples: beige, black, cyan blue, dark brown, gray, green, light green, orange, red, white, yellow
Comparative/superlative forms (blacker, reddest) are not permitted.

**18. Damage terms**
Types of defects, degradation, and malfunctions.
Examples: buckle, chafing, corrosion, crack, deformation, dent, discoloration, distortion, erosion, fracture, fraying, kink, nick, scratch, stain

**19. Computer science, information and communication technology**
Technological concepts, systems, and components.
Examples: AI, authentication, backup, chatbot, cursor, cybersecurity, database, deep learning, email, file, firewall, HTML, icon, internet, laptop, machine learning, network, operating system, screen, smartphone, token, toolbar

**20. Civil and military operations**
Concepts, activities, service delivery, and product management.
Examples: armed forces, combat plan, contractor, customer, deployment, ejection seat, end item, mission, Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM)

**21. Law and regulations**
Legal and regulatory texts, contracts, warranties, and certificates.
Examples: contract, compliance, court, damages, law, legislation, scope, signature, waiver

**22. Animals, plants, and other life forms**
Biological entities in technical contexts.
Examples: bacteria, bird, cat, dog, fungi, horse, insect, moss, rose, termite

---

### How to Use Technical Noun Categories

When you find a word that is not in the approved dictionary:

1. Identify what the word refers to (a part, a tool, a material, a concept, etc.)
2. Find the category that matches that reference
3. If the word fits a category, it is permitted as a technical noun
4. If it does not fit any category, replace it with an approved alternative

Example: "Backup" is not in the approved dictionary. But it is a technical noun in category 19 (computer science). "Do the backup of the computer at regular intervals" is STE-compliant.

Example: "Main landing gear" - "main" is not approved and its dictionary alternative is "primary". But "main landing gear" is a technical noun in category 2. You cannot replace "main" with "primary" here because "primary landing gear" is not the correct technical noun.

## Technical Verbs (Rule 1.12)

A technical verb is a verb term that refers to a specified concept or process. Like technical nouns, they are not in the dictionary. They must obey the same verb-form rules as approved verbs (Section 3 of `rules.md`).

### 4 Technical Verb Categories

**1. Manufacturing processes**
| Subcategory | Examples |
|---|---|
| Remove material | drill, grind, mill, ream, unsolder |
| Add material | flame, insulate, remetal, retread |
| Attach material | braze, crimp, solder, weld |
| Change mechanical strength/structure | anneal, cure, freeze, heat-treat, magnetize, normalize |
| Change surface finish | buff, burnish, dress, passivate, plate, polish |
| Change shape | blend, cast, extrude, spin, stamp |

**2. Computer processes and applications**
| Subcategory | Examples |
|---|---|
| Input and output | click, digitize, enter, press, print, swipe, tap, type |
| User interface and applications | clear, close, copy, cut, delete, deselect, disable, drag, enable, encrypt, filter, highlight, maximize, minimize, navigate, open, paste, save, scroll, sort, validate, zoom |
| System operations | abort, boot, debug, download, format, install, load, process, reboot, update, upload |

**3. Subject-field instructions and information**
| Subcategory | Examples |
|---|---|
| Engineering, math, science | bisect, compensate for, convert, detect, float, modulate, radiate, transform |
| Medical | disinfect, intubate, operate, prescribe, sanitize, sterilize |
| Civil and military | aim, arm, detect, disable, enable, explode, fire, inhibit, intercept, load, lock on, parachute |
| Navigation | approach, descend, deviate, fly, hover, land, navigate, respond, take off, taxi, trim |
| Automotive and railway | accelerate, brake, couple, crank, decouple, dispatch, inflate, park, steer |
| Energy, oil, and gas | compress, distill, drill, emit, extract, inject, pump |

**4. Law and regulations**
For legal and regulatory texts only.
Examples: acknowledge, comply with, conform to, describe, enforce, explain, inform, modify, notify, regulate, sign, supersede, waive

---

### How to Use Technical Verb Categories

If an approved verb in the dictionary can accurately give the instruction, use the approved verb instead of a technical verb. Technical verbs are a fallback for when the approved dictionary lacks the precision you need.

Example: "Detect" is not approved and cannot be a technical verb in all contexts.
- Non-STE: "If you detect broken wires, repair them." → "If you find broken wires, repair them."
- STE: "The security scanner detects metal objects." - "Detect" is a technical verb in category 3 (engineering) in this context.

Do not use a technical verb when an approved verb plus a technical noun can express the same meaning.
- Non-STE: "Clamp the cable in position." → "Put clamps on the cable to hold it in position."
- Non-STE: "Grease the fasteners." → "Apply grease to the fasteners."

## Known Approved-Word Rulings (Cross-Reference)

These are well-established rulings that appear in secondary sources and help illustrate the one-word-one-meaning principle:

| Word | Status | Approved meaning / Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| start (v) | Approved | - |
| begin, commence, initiate (v) | Not approved | → START (v) |
| originate (v) | Not approved | → SOURCE (n) (e.g. "If the valve is the source of the leak...") |
| fall (v) | Approved | "move down by gravity" only |
| check (n) | Approved (noun only) | - |
| check (v) | Not approved | → do a check of / make sure |
| about (prep) | Approved | "concerned with" only |
| approximately (adv) | Approved | the replacement for the "about [a number]" sense |
| around (prep) | Approved | the replacement for the "about [an axis]" sense |
| close (v) | Approved | (1) move to stop passage, (2) operate a circuit breaker |
| follow (v) | Approved | "come after, go after" only |
| obey (v) | Approved | "do what an instruction tells you" |
| can (v) | Approved | auxiliary modal: to be possible, to be able to, or to be permitted to |
| must (v) | Approved | auxiliary modal for requirements |
| may (v) | Not approved | → CAN (v) |
| shall (v) | Not approved | → MUST (v) |
| should (v) | Not approved | → MUST (v) |
| would (v) | Not approved | → CAN (v) |
| might (v) | Not approved | → CAN (v) (a modal; not in the approved dictionary) |
| could (v) | Restricted | a form of CAN; do not use COULD to show possibility |
| rotate (v) | Not approved | → turn |
| accessible (adj) | Not approved | → "can get access to" |
| acceptable (adj) | Not approved | → permitted |
| operable (adj) | Not approved | → "can operate" |

## Transformation Patterns

These sentence-level patterns show how dictionary principles change the text:

| Non-STE | STE | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| "Before acceptance of the unit..." | "Before you accept the unit..." | Nominalization → verb |
| "Rotate the cover until the jacks are accessible." | "Turn the cover until you can get access to the jacks." | Non-approved verb → approved verb; adjective → verb phrase |
| "The unit must be installed carefully." | "Install the unit carefully." | Modal passive → imperative active |
| "The volume control can be adjusted." | "Adjust the volume control." (procedural) or "You can adjust the volume control." (descriptive) | Modal passive → imperative or "you can" |
| "Oil the steel surfaces." | "Apply oil to the steel surfaces." | Technical noun used as verb → technical noun + approved verb |
| "Test the system for leaks." | "Do a test of the system for leaks." or "Do a leak test of the system." | Unapproved verb → approved noun + verb |

## Spelling

Use American English spelling (Merriam-Webster). Common conversions:

| British | American |
|---|---|
| colour | color |
| centre | center |
| organise | organize |
| fibre | fiber |
| litre | liter |
| metre | meter |
| behaviour | behavior |
| labelled | labeled |
| modelling | modeling |
| licence (n) | license |

Exception: if an official directive (contract, style guide, other standard) specifies a different spelling, follow that directive. Do not change quoted text from placards, displays, or labels.
